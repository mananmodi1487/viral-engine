# Case studies

Long-form analyses that combine several records into one argument.

A case study here is held to a higher standard than a record, because it *argues*. It must:

1. State its claim before the evidence, not after.
2. Show the records it is built from, by path.
3. Include at least one record that **contradicts** the claim.
4. State what evidence would falsify the claim.
5. Say how the artifacts were selected — and whether that selection could be biased.

Point 3 is where most case studies fail. A case study assembled from agreeing examples is
not an argument, it is a bibliography with a thesis pasted on top.

## Existing studies

The two files in `examples/` at the repository root (`case-study-1.md`,

here as **non-conforming**, which is more useful than quietly leaving them out.

## What a conformant case study looks like

```
# Claim

<One sentence, stated up front, falsifiable.>

# Evidence

<Records, by path. For each: what it shows, and its label.>

# The record that does not fit

<At least one. If there is none, the claim is probably not interesting.>

# Selection

<How these artifacts were found. What would have been included but was not, and why.>

# What would prove this wrong

<An observable, a horizon, and who could check it.>

# Limitations

<Including: who wrote this, and what they gain if you believe it.>
```
