# Hook patterns

Opening lines, what they were trying to do, and what is known about the result.

The point of this directory is not "best openings". It is a catalogue of *mechanisms* with
the evidence attached, so that a copywriter can choose a mechanism knowingly instead of
copying a line that worked for someone else in a different category.

## The six mechanisms

| Mechanism | What it does | Fails when | Honesty risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Negative comparison | Names the thing you are not, so the reader can locate you instantly | the "not" is a straw man nobody believed in | low, if the comparison is real |
| Concrete number | A specific figure earns belief and stops the skim | the number is invented | **high** — the single most common invented statistic |
| Named enemy | Gives the reader a thing to be against | the enemy is a person or a group instead of a practice | **high** — this is where outrage bait enters |
| Compressed loss | States what the reader is currently losing | there is no loss they recognise | low |
| Receipt | "I built this because I missed two interviews in a week" | the receipt is decorative rather than causal | medium — a true story used to imply causation it cannot support |
| Absurd specificity | "7 tabs, 6 companies, 1 spreadsheet" | it is merely quirky and carries no information | low, if the numbers are true |

Every generated hook in this tool must name its mechanism, and must be checked against the
honesty firewall before it is shown. The mechanism is the useful part; the words are
disposable.

## What is not here

- **No hook library.** A list of lines that worked is a list of lines that worked *once, for
  someone else, in a category you are not in*. Copying it is the single most common way a
  good tool produces generic copy.
- **No engagement numbers.** We do not have impression data, and any figure claiming to
  measure "hook performance" across the internet is invented.
- **No A/B results.** Nothing in this directory was tested against a control. `Experiment
  Weaver` designs those tests; until someone runs them, this is vocabulary, not evidence.

## Contributing

Submit a mechanism with (a) the artifact, (b) the line, (c) what you could observe, and
(d) an `anecdotal` label. If you have a control, you have something much more valuable —
open a PR and label it `reproducible`.
