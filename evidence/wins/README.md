# Wins

Things that spread, with the numbers that exist and the confounds that existed.

## Read this before any file in here

**There are not enough failures in this directory's sibling.** Wins are submitted; failures
are deleted. If you count files across `wins/` and `failures/`, you are measuring who felt
like writing, not what works.

**Every win is a sample of one.** The most common honest outcome statement is: "it worked and
we do not know which part mattered."

**The pattern may be the category, not the method.** A developer tool posted on Hacker News
has a different baseline than a ceramics course posted on Instagram. Cross-category
comparison is the most common error in this whole field.

## What a record here must contain

| Field | Why it is mandatory |
|---|---|
| what was published | So a reader can judge whether it resembles their thing at all |
| the numbers, with source and date | "It did well" is not a record |
| `confounded_by` | What else changed. Usually everything. |
| what cannot be concluded | At least three statements |
| a falsifiable "next time" | So the lesson can lose |

## Current records

| Record | Label | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `2026-04-python-binding-found-two-bugs.md` | reproducible | A win for the *method*: a second implementation found two real bugs. Included here as the example of what a well-labelled record looks like. |

Note the shape of the one record present: it is a win about process, with a mechanism, a
number, and a section explaining what it does not prove. That is the template. A record that
says "our launch went viral, here is how" will be rejected unless it carries numbers and
confounds, and even then it will be labelled `anecdotal` unless someone else can check it.
