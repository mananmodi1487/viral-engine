# Viral Engine

## What this skill does

Takes one input — a topic, product, piece of content, or rough idea — and returns a complete, executable plan for organic spread: the hook, the frame, the structure, the distribution, and the measurement loop.

It is **not** a hype generator. Virality is a distribution property, not a copywriting trick. This skill optimizes for the one thing you can actually control: **making each share a rational, low-cost, high-status act for the person doing it.**

## Core principle

> People do not share things because they are good.
> People share things because sharing them **makes the sharer look good**, **makes them feel something**, or **makes them useful to someone they know**.

Every recommendation must answer: *Why would a specific person risk their reputation to post this?* If you can't name that person and that reason, the idea isn't ready.

---

## Phase 0 — Intake (ask only what you actually need)

Infer as much as possible from the input. If the user hasn't specified, assume:

- **Audience**: the narrowest group who would care most (not "everyone")
- **Goal**: attention → then signups/sales (pick the plausible one)
- **Format**: whatever the platform rewards (short video, text post, README, landing page)

Ask at most **2 questions**, and only if the answer changes the output materially:

1. Who specifically is this for? (One sentence, e.g. "second-year CS students in India cramming for placements")
2. What's the one action you want after they see it? (star, sign up, buy, reply, share)

If the user says "just do it," invent a specific audience and state the assumption at the top of the output.

### Identify the object type

| Type | Success looks like |
|---|---|
| Repo / tool | Stars, forks, "I use this daily" replies |
| Post / thread | Replies, quote-posts, saves |
| Product / app | Signups with retention, word-of-mouth invites |
| Campaign | Hashtag/format adoption by strangers |
| Video | Completion + shares to a specific person ("this is you") |

---

## Phase 1 — Diagnose the spreadability

Score the input 1–5 on each. Be honest; low scores are information, not failure.

| Lever | Question | Weak signal | Strong signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Surprise** | Does it violate an expectation in one sentence? | "helps you be productive" | "the app your professor doesn't want you to use" |
| **Identity** | Does sharing it say something about the sharer? | generic utility | "I'm the kind of person who knows this" |
| **Usefulness** | Can someone forward it to a named person? | "interesting" | "send this to your teammate who does X" |
| **Emotion** | Does it produce a spike (awe, outrage, relief, recognition)? | mild interest | "wait, that's real?" |
| **Cost to share** | Is it 1 tap, no explanation needed? | requires a paragraph of context | self-explanatory in a screenshot |
| **Tribal fit** | Is there an in-group that will defend it? | everyone | a specific community with a shared enemy |
| **Remixability** | Can others make their own version? | closed | template, format, or dataset they can fork |

**The weakest lever is the bottleneck.** Fix that one; improving an already-strong lever is wasted effort.

---

## Phase 2 — Build the hook

Generate **12 hooks, then keep 3.** Never present one hook — the user must be able to choose.

### The 7 hook patterns

1. **Curiosity gap** — withhold the mechanism, not the payoff.
   *"The 3-minute habit 10,000 professionals use to beat burnout."*
2. **Forbidden / insider** — imply gatekeeping being violated.
   *"The app my professor doesn't want you to use (but your GPA depends on it)."*
3. **Paradox** — two things that shouldn't coexist.
   *"I cut my workweek to 20 hours and shipped twice as much."*
4. **Enemy** — name the thing everyone privately resents.
   *"Your habit tracker is designed to make you quit."*
5. **Receipt** — lead with a number someone can verify or envy.
   *"0 → 12,000 users in 21 days. Zero ad spend. Here's the exact loop."*
6. **Reframe** — same fact, new category.
   *"It's not a meditation app. It's reputation management."*
7. **Direct address** — call out one person so precisely it feels invasive.
   *"If you have 47 browser tabs open, this is about you."*

### Hook rules

- **≤ 12 words** for the first line. The second line does the work.
- **No adjectives doing the heavy lifting.** "Powerful," "revolutionary," "game-changing" are dead weight.
- **Specific > clever.** Numbers, names, timeframes, constraints.
- **One claim per hook.** Two claims = zero claims.
- **Truth constraint:** every number must be real or clearly framed as illustrative. Fabricated stats are how projects die in the replies.

Deliver 3 hooks with a one-line note on which audience each targets.

## Phase 3 — Position it (the paradox frame)

Positioning is choosing the **category** the thing competes in. The same object can be boring in one category and urgent in another.

1. List the **obvious category** — the one everyone defaults to. Label it "crowded."
2. List **3 adjacent categories** with the same underlying benefit but a different buyer, budget, or emotion.
3. Pick the frame where the object is **most unusual and most needed**.
4. Write the **positioning sentence**: *"It's not [obvious category]. It's [new category] — for [specific person] who [specific situation]."*

### Positioning traps

- **Too clever**: the frame needs explaining → drop it.
- **Too broad**: "for everyone" = for no one. Narrow until it stings.
- **Category with no budget/attention**: "It's a lifestyle" sells nothing.
- **Dishonest stretch**: if the product can't survive the claim, the frame becomes the story (and not in a good way).

---

## Phase 4 — Structure for shares

Design the object so that sharing is built into its shape, not bolted on afterward.

### The 6 structural share mechanisms

| Mechanism | What it does | How to build it |
|---|---|---|
| **Template / fork** | Lets people make their own version | Ship a copyable format, not just an output |
| **Named pattern** | Gives people vocabulary to cite you | Brand the technique ("the 3-Second Rule") |
| **Public artifact** | Shareable proof exists | Screenshot-ready output, badge, score, card |
| **Completion invite** | Sharing is a required step | "Add your result," "submit yours," leaderboard |
| **Tribal marker** | Signals belonging | A stance, a mascot, a shared enemy |
| **Built-in gift** | Forwarding helps someone specific | "Send this to the one person who…" |

Pick **2** — three or more reads as desperate. Explain the cost to the sharer for each and how you lowered it.

### Content skeleton (adapt to format)

```
Line 1     — hook (pattern, ≤12 words)
Line 2-3   — the tension / why now
Middle     — 3 concrete proofs (number, story or demo, mechanism)
Turn       — the reframe that makes it feel like insider knowledge
Close      — one specific action, no hedging
```

For products, the skeleton becomes: **one job done extremely well → visible result in <60s → one reason to tell someone.**

## Phase 5 — Distribution

Virality is a loop, not a launch. Design the loop.

1. **Seed nodes** — 5–10 named communities/people where the specific audience already gathers. Not "Twitter." Name subreddits, Discords, newsletters, group chats, conferences, professors, maintainers.
2. **The first 100** — how do the first 100 people arrive? (Personal DMs, a single well-placed post, a demo in an existing community.) Be concrete; "post on social media" is not a plan.
3. **The share trigger** — the exact sentence a fan would use when forwarding it. Write it for them.
4. **The loop** — what does a share produce that creates another share? (New remix, visible usage count, answer that generates a question, member's own result.)
5. **The backstop** — the one durable channel that survives if the spike doesn't happen (search, newsletter, docs, integration).

**Anti-patterns**: launch-day mass DMs; posting the same text to 12 subreddits; asking for stars without giving a reason; buying engagement; promising what the artifact doesn't deliver.

---

## Phase 6 — Measure & iterate

Never report "it went viral" or "it flopped" without a number attached.

| Metric | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Hook rate** (3-sec view / scroll-past ratio) | Is the first line working? |
| **Save / star ratio** to views | Is it worth keeping, not just reading? |
| **Share ratio** (shares ÷ views) | The actual virality signal |
| **Reply sentiment** (first 20 replies, manually read) | Whether the frame landed or backfired |
| **Conversion** (view → signup/star/buy) | Whether attention is worth anything |
| **7-day retention** | Whether the loop has substance or is a spike |

**The iteration rule:** change **one** variable per cycle — hook, frame, structure, or channel. Changing several at once teaches you nothing.

Decision gates:
- Strong hook, no shares → the share trigger is missing (Phase 4).
- Shares but no conversion → wrong audience or wrong close (Phase 3/5).
- Good numbers, hostile replies → the claim overreached; fix honesty before reach.
- Nothing at all → the bottleneck lever in Phase 1 is the problem, not the copy.

---

## Output format

Always return these 8 sections, in order, in markdown:

```
1. THE BET          — one paragraph: what this is, who it's for, why it spreads
2. DIAGNOSIS        — the 7-lever table with the bottleneck called out
3. HOOKS            — 3 hooks, each with target audience + which pattern
4. POSITIONING      — "It's not X. It's Y." + the crowded category you left
5. STRUCTURE        — 2 share mechanisms + the content skeleton, filled in
6. DISTRIBUTION     — seed nodes, first 100, the share sentence, the loop, the backstop
7. FIRST 48 HOURS   — a numbered, time-boxed checklist the user can execute today
8. SCORECARD        — 3 metrics to watch, with the number that means "working"
```

Keep it dense. No preamble, no motivational filler, no "in today's fast-paced world."

## Honesty guardrails (non-negotiable)

- **Never invent statistics, testimonials, or users.** If you need a number and don't have one, write `[your real number]` and say what to measure.
- **Never fabricate social proof.** Fake testimonials in a README are the fastest route to a public callout.
- **No guaranteed outcomes.** You cannot promise 100K stars, 12,000 users, or virality. You can promise a better-designed attempt.
- **Flag the failure mode.** Every plan gets one line: "This fails if ___."
- **No engagement bait that costs the user credibility** — no fake controversy, no manufactured outrage, no astroturfing. Short-term reach, long-term reputation loss; the trade is almost never worth it.
- **Respect platforms and communities.** Read the rules of any subreddit/Discord before recommending a post. Self-promo bans are real.

---

## Quality bar

Before returning, check:

- [ ] Could a stranger explain the hook to a friend in one sentence?
- [ ] Is the audience specific enough that you could name 5 real communities?
- [ ] Does every number have a real source or an explicit placeholder?
- [ ] Is there exactly one call to action?
- [ ] Would *you* share this from your own account? If not, say why.
- [ ] Is the bottleneck lever addressed, or just the fun ones?
- [ ] Does the first 48 hours contain only things doable by one person without a budget?
