## Why a research folder at all

Because "this makes things go viral" is the most confidently asserted and least supported claim in marketing. The alternative isn't silence — it's being specific about what's established, what's inferred, and what's just a decent guess.

Three rules govern this folder:

1. **Every finding gets a citation** — author, year, venue.
2. **Inference is labeled as inference.** "The research suggests" is not "the research proves."
3. **Limits are written down.** The "what the research does *not* say" section exists for a reason: most of this work is on news articles and Twitter cascades, in English, largely US-centric. Transfer to your niche is an assumption you should test.

---

## How to cite this repo

If you use a pattern from here, cite the **original source**, not this repo. This is a synthesis; the findings belong to the researchers. Full attributions are in [CREDITS.md](../CREDITS.md).

The one framing that's original to this repo is [The Broadcaster Rule](virality-patterns.md#3-the-broadcaster-rule-) — a design inference drawn from Goel et al.'s diffusion data, not a finding of theirs.

## Found an error?

Open an issue. Misquoted findings, wrong years, superseded results, and places where this repo states an inference as a result are all high-priority corrections. Getting this right matters more than the repo looking confident.
