# 📖 Case Study: the repo you're reading

**Subject:** this repository.
**Method:** ran Viral Engine on itself.
**Status:** results pending. This document will be updated with real numbers or it will be updated with an honest account of what failed.

> **Read this first.** Every case study in the growth-tooling space claims a 10x result. This one can't, because it hasn't happened yet — the repo is days old. ## The input

**What it is:** a single-file Claude Skill that turns a topic, product, or rough idea into a launch plan — hook, category, share structure, distribution, and a scorecard.

**Who it's for:** creators, indie founders, and marketers who can build the thing but can't get it in front of people.

**The one action:** run it once on something you actually published.

## Phase 1 — The diagnosis (run on itself)

Scoring your own artifact is uncomfortable. Here's the honest version.

| Lever | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Surprise** | 3/5 | "A tool that makes things go viral" is not surprising. It's a crowded category with a credibility problem. |
| **Identity** | 4/5 | Strong. Sharing a *specific* reframe ("it's not a habit tracker, it's placement-risk management") makes the sharer look like they understand marketing. |
| **Usefulness** | 4/5 | High for the exact reader with something launching. Zero for everyone else. |
| **Emotion** | 2/5 | **This is the weakest lever.** A clever reframe produces interest, not arousal. Nobody's pulse changes. |
| **Cost to share** | 5/5 | Excellent. A screenshot of a before/after is self-explanatory and needs no caption. |
| **Tribal fit** | 3/5 | There's a tribe of indie builders and Claude users, but they don't have a shared enemy. |
| **Remixability** | 4/5 | High — the templates folder and the skill file itself can be forked and adapted. |

**Named bottleneck: EMOTION (2/5).**

This is the finding that changed the repo. The original instinct was to add more features and more badges. That would have optimized strong levers and left the actual problem untouched.

**What the bottleneck forces instead:** the emotional payload can't be the tool (tools don't arouse). It has to be the *before/after moment* — the specific second where someone sees their boring product reframed as something urgent. That's a recognition spike, which is a mild form of arousal, and it's the only one available here. So every asset in this repo is built around that one moment: the hero animation, the before/after card, the examples.

## Phase 2 — The positioning

```
It's not a "viral content" tool.

It's a spread diagnostic — for people who already published something
and watched it do nothing.
```

**Category left behind:** "AI marketing tools." Crowded, distrusted, associated with spam and fake case studies.

**Category entered:** diagnostics. Boring-sounding, high-credibility, and it licenses the tool to say "your idea isn't ready" — which is the behavior that makes the output trustworthy.

**The trade this costs, stated plainly:** diagnostics don't sound exciting, and "spread diagnostic" will never trend. That's the price of the credibility.

---

## Phase 3 — The share structure

Two mechanisms, chosen from six. Not three.

**1. Named pattern** — *The Broadcaster Rule.*
Most virality advice says maximize the share rate. The diffusion research says popularity tracks the *largest single broadcast*. Naming this gives people a phrase to repeat, and it contradicts advice they've already heard — which is what makes a named pattern worth citing.

*Cost to sharer:* none. *Why it's shareable:* it makes the sharer look like they know something the standard advice doesn't.

**2. Template / fork** — the `/templates` folder.
Not an output to admire, but a set of blanks to fill in. Templates travel because they're usable, not just readable.

*Cost to sharer:* one copy-paste. *Why it's shareable:* "here, use this" is the easiest forward there is.

**Rejected: tribal marker.** Inventing an enemy for this repo would have been effective and dishonest. It was cut on purpose.

## Phase 5 — This document is the case study, and that's the point

Here's the honest bit about a repo that claims to teach virality:

**The most persuasive thing it can do is show its own reasoning, including the parts that are unflattering.**

- The emotion score is 2/5 and it's written down
- The bottleneck forced a redesign, and that's documented
- The category choice costs excitement, and that's stated
- The results section is empty, because the results don't exist yet

A case study written *before* the outcome can only be honest about the reasoning. That's a weaker artifact than a triumphant one, and a much more useful one — because you can follow the logic and disagree with it at any step.

## What would count as working

Pre-committed, so it can't be rationalized later.

| Timeframe | Metric | Working | Not working |
|---|---|---|---|
| Week 2 | Strangers who ran it | ≥ 10 | Anything from my own network only |
| Week 4 | Outputs posted publicly | ≥ 5 | People use it privately and never mention it |
| Week 4 | Star-to-run conversion | ≥ 20% | Runs without stars → the README isn't asking |
| Month 3 | Returning users | ≥ 15% | A novelty, not a habit |
| Month 3 | Stars | ≥ 1,000 | See [SUMMARY.md](SUMMARY.md) for why the target isn't 100K |

**And the failure condition, written down before launch:**

> This fails if the output reads as generic. A tool that returns "be consistent and provide value" produces no screenshots, and with no screenshots the entire share mechanism — the before/after — collapses. Specificity isn't a style choice here. It's the load-bearing wall.

## What you can copy from this

Whether or not this repo succeeds:

1. **Score your own thing before you market it.** The weak lever is usually not the one you're working on.
2. **Position before you write.** A new category generates hooks; a new hook can't rescue a dead category.
3. **Pick two share mechanisms.** Not one, not five.
4. **Write the failure condition first.** It converts a hope into a test.
5. **Leave the results blank until they're real.** The blank is more persuasive than a number you made up, and it survives contact with someone who checks.
