# 15 — Teaching this to someone else

**30 minutes.** The final module, and the only one whose exercise cannot be faked.

## Why teaching is the last module

Everything before this trains a judgement. Judgement that has only been applied to your own work
is half-trained, because you have too much context on your own artifact to notice what a stranger
would see. Running the whole process on someone else's work — and having them run it on yours —
is where the method stops being a checklist and becomes a skill.

It is also the mechanism by which this repository survives. A free tool with one author has a
lifespan of one author.

## The protocol

Pair with one person. Ninety minutes, split three ways.

**Minutes 0-20 — they describe, you listen.**
They tell you what they made. Say nothing. Write down every question you want to ask. Do not ask
them yet. This is the single hardest part and the reason the protocol exists.

**Minutes 20-40 — you describe it back.**
Say what you understood: the category, the audience, the job, the reason someone would forward it.
They correct you. Every correction is a finding, and you write it down verbatim.

**Minutes 40-90 — run the engine together, on their artifact.**
Not on yours. Theirs. Run `analyze`, read the highest-leverage dimension, and take the improvement
action seriously. Then switch roles if there is time.

## The rules for the listener

- Do not explain what they should have done. Ask what they tried.
- When you do not understand something, say "I did not understand that", not "you should clarify".
  The first is information; the second is a review.
- If their description is good, say exactly which sentence did the work. It is the most useful
  sentence you can give a person who is about to launch, and nobody says it.

## The rules for the author

- Do not defend. Every "well, what it actually does is…" is a data point about the description,
  not about the listener.
- Write corrections down. You will forget them within an hour and they are the whole output.

## What "done" looks like

Not "we finished the modules". Done is:

- You can explain, in two minutes, why chain-style virality is a bad target and what replaces it.
- You have written a repositioning sentence that survived all three tests, and had someone confirm
  it means what you think it means.
- You have a failure condition with an observable and a horizon, written down before the result.
- You have run one test that changed a decision.
- You have decided, explicitly, whether to launch.

## Exercise

`../exercises/15-run-a-pair-session.md`

Run the protocol. Then write two paragraphs in `evidence/` — one about what the other person's
artifact taught you about your own, and one about what the engine got wrong. Submit the second
as an issue with the artifact and the disagreement. The evaluation dataset is built from those.

## What this module cannot tell you

- Whether your judgement is good, as opposed to consistent. Consistency is achievable alone;
  calibrate against measured outcomes in `evidence/` and see.
- Whether teaching it helps you. It does, but that is a claim from other fields and it has not
  been tested here. If you run a session and it does not help, that is a `failure` record.

## After this

There is no certificate. There is a template in `certificates-transparent/` for a record of what
you did, self-issued, carrying no accreditation, which is exactly what it is. The thing you have
that is worth anything is a sentence someone else understood.
