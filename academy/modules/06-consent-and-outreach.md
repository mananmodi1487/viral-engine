# 06 — Consent and the ethics of outreach

**30 minutes.** The module that decides whether the rest of this is worth anything.

## The line

There is a difference between these two sentences:

> "I made a thing that might help your readers, here it is, no action needed."

> "Hey! Loved your content. Would mean a lot if you could share my launch with your audience.
> Happy to reciprocate!"

The second is not evil. It is a small, ordinary, extremely common transaction. It is also the
first step toward a world where everyone's inbox is a negotiation, and the people who are
worst at asking — the shy, the non-native speaker, the person without social capital — never
get heard.

The rule that follows: **you may tell someone a thing exists. You may not ask them to amplify
it.** If it is useful, they will. If it is not, an ask buys you one resentful post and a
permanent reputation cost.

## The message with no ask in it

Four sentences, and none of them is a request:

```
Subject: a tool for [their readers' specific problem]

[Their context, showing you know what they cover, in one clause.]

I built [thing], which does [the job, in their vocabulary]. It is free and offline-capable.

No action needed — sending it in case it is useful for your [newsletter/team/course].
If it is not, no reply is the right reply.
```

Why it works better than an ask, instrumentally:

- It respects that they are busy, which is the trait they will remember.
- It gives them the option to use it later, when their need arises, without a social debt.
- If they do post it, the post is *theirs*, which is the only kind that reads as genuine.

## What is off limits, without exception

- **Mass sending.** No list, no queue, no automation. The engine's `max_contacts` is 3 and the
  broadcaster analysis refuses to produce a list of individuals at all.
- **Scraped addresses.** Public is not consent.
- **Personalisation by surveillance.** Do not open someone's private life to find an angle.
- **Reciprocity deals.** "I will share yours if you share mine" is an engagement pod with two
  members, and the audience is the product.
- **Following up twice.** One message. Not one message and a "just bumping this".

## The test that settles most cases

> Would you be comfortable if this message, and the fact you sent it, were published on your
> own timeline with your name attached?

If yes, send it. If no, the ask is doing the work, and the ask is the problem.

Write the message for one of your broadcaster roles from module 05. Then run the honesty

## What this module cannot tell you

- Which communities consider any self-linking a violation. Read each community's rules; they
  differ, they change, and the tool cannot read them for you.
- Whether someone will perceive a no-ask message as a covert ask. Some will. That is a cost of
  having an inbox, not a flaw in the approach.
