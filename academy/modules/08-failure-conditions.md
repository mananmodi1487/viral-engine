# 08 — Failure conditions

**30 minutes.** The single highest-leverage habit in the academy.

## The problem with "I'll see how it goes"

"I'll see how it goes" feels humble. It is actually a way of avoiding a decision. The result
arrives, and then you interpret it: a slow week was the algorithm, a quiet thread was bad
timing, and the interpretation is always available because you never wrote down what would
count as failure.

A failure condition is a pre-commitment. It is the thing that makes the result mean something.

## The three parts (all mandatory)

| Part | Question | Bad | Good |
|---|---|---|---|
| `statement` | What would prove the attempt wrong? | "low traction" | "fewer than 20 forwards from people who are not me" |
| `observable` | What would a stranger see? | "people did not get it" | "in reply threads, three people describe it as a to-do app" |
| `horizon` | By when? | "eventually" | "14 days after publishing" |

A failure condition without an **observable** is a feeling. Without a **horizon** it can never
fire, which makes it decoration.

## Four conditions every launch should have

1. **Comprehension.** Do readers describe it the way you do? If they use a different category,
   the copy failed — not the market.
2. **Forwardability.** Did anyone with no relationship to you pass it on? Not like — forward.
3. **Fitness for the claimed job.** Did anyone use it for the job you named? If they use it for
   a different job, you have found a better product than the one you launched.
4. **Your own threshold.** The number below which you will stop, stated before you can see the
   number. This is the one people avoid writing, because writing it means the decision is made.

## The pre-mortem version

Before launching, complete: *"It is [date + 14 days]. This failed. The single most likely
reason, in hindsight, was ______."*

Then make that sentence into a failure condition with an observable. You have just converted
your worst private worry into a test. This is more useful than optimism, and considerably
cheaper than a postmortem written when it is too late to change anything.

## Why this raises your score honestly

The index gives weight to `failure_awareness` (50 points) and requires a failure condition on
every dimension. That is not a scoring trick. A document that states its failure conditions is
cheaper for a reader to evaluate, and readers forward things that are cheap to evaluate.

## What this module cannot tell you

- What a realistic threshold is for your category. Nobody knows; base rates are not public.
- Whether your horizon is long enough. Almost certainly it is too short — most things take
  far longer than the launch week.
