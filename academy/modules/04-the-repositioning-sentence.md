# 04 — The repositioning sentence

**30 minutes.** One sentence. The hardest writing task in the academy.

## The pattern

> It's not **X**. It's **Y**.

X is the category the reader would put you in by default. Y is the category you are actually
in. The sentence works because it does the reader's categorisation work *for* them, in the two
seconds they were going to spend doing it themselves.

## When it is a gimmick

The pattern has been copied into meaninglessness. Three failure modes:

**1. Straw man X.** "It's not just another social network. It's a movement."
Nobody thought it was a social network. The X must be the category a real reader would
genuinely assign, otherwise you have spent your only sentence arguing with nobody.

**2. Ornamental Y.** "It's not a notebook. It's a *second brain*."
"Second brain" is a label, not a category. It maps to no job, so the reader has learned only
that you use different words for notebook.

**3. Untrue Y.** "It's not a CRM. It's a revenue intelligence platform."
If a reader who knows CRMs looked inside and found a CRM, the sentence was a lie, and the cost
lands on whoever forwarded it.

## The three-part test

Your sentence survives if:

1. **A reader in category X would agree they had put you there.** (X is real.)
2. **Category Y names a job, not a descriptor.** (Fill the blank: "I use this when I need to
   ______" — the blank works.)
3. **The artifact already does Y.** (Not "will do".)

## Examples, with what makes them work

| Sentence | X real? | Y is a job? | Artifact matches? |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Not a habit tracker. Placement-risk management for students in placement season." | yes | yes — which deadline will I miss | yes |
| "Not a analytics dashboard. The five minutes before a launch when you decide whether to postpone." | yes | partly — "five minutes" is a moment, not a job | arguable |
| "Not a productivity app. A deadline-risk tool for freelancers with six clients." | yes | yes | yes |
| "Not a note app. A thinking partner." | yes | no | no |

The third row is plain and works. The fourth is beautiful and fails all three tests. **Plain
and true beats beautiful and unfalsifiable**, every time, because the reader's job is to decide
whether to read on, not to admire you.

## A warning about this exact sentence

The `analyze` command will tell you whether it detected a category pairing, and that is a
structural check — it looks for the pattern, not for whether the pattern is *good*. A sentence
can pass the check and fail all three tests above. The engine cannot tell the difference
between a real reposition and a slogan.

That is not a limitation to work around. It is the reason this module has no automated answer:
writing the sentence is the work.

## Exercise

`../exercises/04-write-the-sentence.md`

Write three versions. Put them to the three tests. Delete two. Then, and this is the part
people skip, show one to someone who does **not** know what you built and ask them one question:
"what do you think this does?" If their answer is not your Y, the sentence is not doing its job
yet — and the fix is usually in X, not in Y.

## What this module cannot tell you

- Whether your Y will be understood by people outside your field. That needs a real reader.
- Whether Y is a category with room in it. Only the market knows.
