# 05 — Who carries it

**25 minutes.** By the end you should be able to name a *category* of broadcaster and explain
why they would want to, without listing a single human being.

## The mental substitution

The instinct is: "who has a big audience in my space". That produces a list of influencers, and
a list of influencers produces mass outreach, and mass outreach produces a reputation.

Replace it with: **"whose readers already have the problem, and whose job it is to bring them
useful things?"**

That question produces a different set:

| Instead of | Consider |
|---|---|
| The person with 200k followers | The person who runs a 900-subscriber newsletter for a specific job |
| A "big account" in the space | The maintainer who writes the changelog people read |
| The influencer | The internal team lead who forwards tools to five people |
| The celebrity | The teacher who needs a worked example |

Small audiences with a reason to share beat large audiences with none. This is the broadcast
finding from module 01 applied: one person deciding to post is the mechanism, and the size of
their audience is secondary to whether the post makes sense for them.

## The four broadcaster types

**The practitioner.** Does the job your tool addresses. Shares because it is useful to their
peers and sharing it makes them look good to those peers. The most common real broadcaster.

**The curator.** Collects and explains. Their whole reputation is "found a good thing and told
you why". They need a *reason*, not a product: if you cannot say why this is interesting in one
sentence they can reuse, they cannot use it.

**The internal forwarder.** Never posts publicly. Forwards inside an organisation. Invisible in
every public metric, and responsible for most real adoption.

**The teacher.** Runs a course, writes a tutorial, needs an example. They will use your thing
even if it is small, because their need is pedagogical and your tool is a good teaching object.

## What a broadcaster brief contains

Never a name. Always:

- **Role**: "the person who writes the internal engineering newsletter at a company of 100-500"
- **Why them**: what their readers get, in their terms
- **How to find them**: the discovery method ("search for newsletters that cover CLI tooling")
  — never a scraped list
- **Consent status**: `not-contacted` until a human has an actual relationship with them
- **Disqualifier**: the condition under which you should not approach this person at all
- **The message**: no ask in it

That last one is module 06.

## The honest limitation

You cannot know whether a broadcaster will care before you ask a real person. Any tool that
claims to predict this is guessing and dressing the guess in data. Broadcast fit is the lowest
basis-quality dimension in the whole index, and the report says so.

## What this module cannot tell you

- Who will actually post it. Nobody knows, including them, until they do.
- Whether the community has a norm against self-promotion that will get you banned. Read the
  rules; the tool cannot.
