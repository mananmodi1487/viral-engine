# 10 — The friction audit

**25 minutes.** Finding the ten seconds that stop a forward.

## The idea

Forwarding has a cost. Always. Even for someone who loves your work, passing it on requires:
recognising who would want it, finding them, composing a sentence, and accepting a small social
risk that the thing is not as good as they said.

You cannot remove that cost. You can remove everything *else* that is in the way.

## The seven frictions, in order of how often they kill a forward

| Friction | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| No one-sentence reason | The forwarder has to explain it themselves | Write the sentence they would paste |
| Unclear audience | They cannot name who to send it to | Name the role, not the market |
| Setup cost | Seven steps before anything works | A demo, a sample, or an install with zero config |
| Unverifiable claim | They would be staking their name on it | Downgrade the claim to its evidence class |
| Broken thing | The link is dead, the repo is empty | Check the first-run path lasts five minutes |
| Unreadable layout | Long prose, no headings, no quotable line | One blockquote they can lift |
| Inaccessible | Screen reader, zoom, contrast fail | Fix before launch, not after |

## The forwardable unit

The most valuable artifact you can produce is **one sentence a reader can paste into a message
with no editing**:

> "This tells you which part of your launch copy will make a stranger close the tab. Free,
> offline, no account."

It works because it contains: the job, the audience, the price, and the cost. The reader adds
"thought of you" and sends it.

If you cannot write that sentence, the description is not yet working, and no amount of
additional explanation will fix it.

## The five-minute test

Give someone your link. Say nothing. Watch for five minutes.

- Where do they stop?
- What do they click first?
- What do they ask you?

Every question they ask is a friction you did not remove. Write them down; do not answer them in
the moment. Then fix the copy.

## The irreducible list

Some friction cannot be fixed by copy, and saying so is part of being honest:

- The reader does not have the problem. No description helps.
- The reader is not the buyer.
- The thing requires trust you have not earned. Time, not copy, fixes this.
- The category does not exist in their head and they are not willing to learn one today.

A tool that promises to optimise any of these is lying. The friction reducer names them and
stops.

## Exercise

`../exercises/10-forwardable-unit.md`

Write your forwardable unit in one sentence, under 25 words. Test: give it to someone with no
context and ask "who would you send this to?" If they cannot name a specific kind of person, the
sentence is not done.

## What this module cannot tell you

- Whether the first-run path actually works on someone else's machine. Test it on a clean
  laptop, or accept the risk honestly.
- Whether your audience's platform punishes external links. Some do.
