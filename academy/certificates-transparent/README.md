# Certificates, transparently

There is no certificate. This directory exists to say so, in the place where a certificate would
otherwise be, because the absence is a feature and should be visible rather than merely missing.

## Why no certificate

A certificate would need to mean something. To mean something it needs an assessment that is not
self-administered, and an assessor that is not the author. Neither exists here.

What exists is `certificate-template.md`: a self-issued record of what you did, carrying no
accreditation, which you may keep, print, or ignore. It is honest about being a record rather than
a credential, and that honesty is why it is worth more than a badge from a tool that grades its
own homework.

## What a real credential would require

If someone wanted to make this into a course that awards something genuinely useful:

1. An assessment that a third party scores, not a checker written by the author.
2. A portfolio requirement — the student's artifact, before and after, reviewed by a person.
3. A published rubric for the review, with inter-rater disagreement recorded.
4. A named assessor who is willing to be wrong in public.

None of those exist. Anything cheaper is a marketing device wearing an academic costume, and this
project's whole argument is against exactly that.

## What you can honestly say

> I completed the fifteen modules of the Viral Engine Commons academy and ran its checkers on my
> own work. There is no accreditation attached.

That is true, and it is checkable, because the checkers are in the repository.
